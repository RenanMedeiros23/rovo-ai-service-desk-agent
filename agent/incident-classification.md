# Incident Classification Rules

## Impact

- High: Critical business processes or multiple departments affected.
- Medium: A department or a group of users affected.
- Low: Individual users or limited business disruption.

## Urgency

- High: Immediate action required due to significant business risk or a critical deadline.
- Medium: Timely resolution required, but business operations can continue.
- Low: Limited time sensitivity and acceptable temporary alternatives.

## Priority Matrix

| Impact / Urgency | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | Highest | High | Medium |
| Medium | High | Medium | Low |
| Low | Medium | Low | Lowest |

## Classification Rules

- Evaluate business impact and urgency before recommending priority.
- Consider affected users, critical services, business deadlines, and available workarounds.
- Request additional information when necessary.
- Explain the reasoning behind every priority recommendation.
- Follow the organization's approved priority matrix when available.
- Require human approval before updating incident priority.

## Example

Incident: SAP unavailable, production stopped, 40 employees affected, and no workaround available.

Impact: High.

Urgency: High.

Recommended priority: Highest.

Recommended action: Immediate investigation and escalation to specialized support.
