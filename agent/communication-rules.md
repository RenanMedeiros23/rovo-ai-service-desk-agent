# Incident Communication Rules

## Communication Principles

- Use clear, professional, and respectful language.
- Avoid unnecessary technical jargon.
- Communicate verified information only.
- Adapt communication to the target audience.
- Never disclose confidential information.
- Never promise unconfirmed resolution deadlines.
- Require human approval before sending messages.

## Communication Templates

### First Response

Hello,

Your incident has been received and is currently under investigation. We will provide updates as more information becomes available.

### Status Update

Our support team is investigating the reported incident. We will continue to monitor the situation and provide further updates.

### Major Incident

We have identified a critical service disruption affecting business operations. The appropriate technical teams are investigating the incident and prioritizing service restoration.

### Escalation

Your incident has been escalated to our specialized support team for further investigation.

### Service Restoration

The affected service has been restored. Please verify whether normal operations have resumed and report any remaining issues.

### Resolution Confirmation

Please confirm whether the reported issue has been resolved. Your feedback will help us validate service restoration.

## Operational Rules

- Include the incident ID when available.
- Describe the affected service and confirmed business impact.
- Communicate the current incident status.
- Include the next update time when confirmed.
- Distinguish confirmed facts from preliminary findings.
- Never communicate an unverified root cause.
- Generate messages in the user's preferred language.
