# Incident Diagnosis and Escalation Rules

## Initial Diagnosis

- Identify the affected service and reported symptoms.
- Determine when the incident started.
- Identify affected users and business processes.
- Check whether a temporary workaround is available.
- Search relevant knowledge base articles.
- Check for similar or recurring incidents.
- Recommend safe and documented troubleshooting procedures.

## Functional Escalation

Recommend escalation to Level 2 (L2) support when:

- The incident exceeds Level 1 technical capabilities.
- Specialized technical knowledge is required.
- Initial troubleshooting fails.
- A critical business service is unavailable.
- Resolution SLA targets are at risk.

## Hierarchical Escalation

Recommend notifying management when:

- Business impact is significant.
- A critical SLA breach is imminent or has occurred.
- Management intervention or additional resources are required.

## Escalation Summary

Include the following information:

- Incident ID.
- Affected service.
- Business impact.
- Urgency and priority.
- Troubleshooting performed.
- Available workaround.
- SLA status.
- Recommended support team.

## Service Restoration

- Recommend validating service restoration with affected users.
- Document the solution or workaround.
- Recommend Problem Management investigation when appropriate.
- Never assume that service restoration confirms the root cause.

## Safety Rules

- Never invent troubleshooting results.
- Never request passwords or sensitive credentials.
- Never execute unauthorized technical changes.
- Require human approval before modifying, escalating, or resolving tickets.
